# Wear OS Compass: interaction redesign (v2)

Date: 2026-10-11
Status: approved by the owner ("Redesign looks great! Proceed") from mockups on the review page.
Amends: `2026-10-11-wear-compass-design.md`. Where the two differ, this document wins. Everything the first spec says that is not changed here still holds, including backup behavior, the supported versions and the testing rules.

## The owner's requests, verbatim

- "waypoint markers should be lines, not dots. The active waypoint should be marked in an arrow (same style as North, but in that waypoint's color)"
- "the process for adding waypoints is too clunky; should just be a single button press. Then to edit or rename, swipe left (or right twice) or long press the watch face to reveal waypoint settings menu. Swipe right (or left twice) to reveal full settings"
- "add a "breadcrumb" mode: double button press to activate, single button press to last down waypoints. Then at a double press, set targets to each waypoint in turn, in reverse chronological order (deleting each as it is reached and then activating the next target). Default: disabled."
- "add another setting to initiate a more powerful vibration when you are facing the current waypoint target (blind navigation mode)"
- "work with designer mode to clean up the menu and appearance of the alternative screens-- need to improve efficiency, appearance, theme consistency, color compliments, and usability. Minimize number of presses/actions are needed to perform each function."

Earlier requests that this redesign must carry forward:

- "If there are no waypoints, just show the compass without waypoints; should be useable as a standard compass without distraction"
- "Hold flat notification should have a nice looping animation showing the watch moving to the correct orientation"
- "the compass should do a very faint tap (subtle vibrate) whenever the orientation crosses one of the minor ticks"
- "Also add option to show current GPS coordinates on the main compass screen (off by default)", at a precision of about 10 m.
- "For names that don't fit without clipping, use marquee back and forth scrolling (in place) to reveal the full text over time"
- "Make sure to test screenshots of all modes and screens"

## Decisions the owner made by answering questions

- Breadcrumbs are a separate temporary trail. Named waypoints are never touched or deleted by it.
- Blind navigation: one strong pulse on turning onto the target, then a soft pulse about once a second while still facing it.
- The crown press is the only way to leave the app. Swipes are for moving between the three screens. (The owner's decision of 2026-10-12, under "Changed by the owner after approval", restates this and says what Back and the side button do.)

## Decisions made in the mockups and approved with them

- Arrow reading: every target is a line across the ring; the focused target adds an arrow shaped like the blue pointer, in the waypoint's color.
- Tapping the middle of the compass no longer opens the waypoint list.
- With the breadcrumb setting on, a single press waits about a third of a second to see whether a second press follows. With it off, a press acts at once.
- Editing a waypoint has no confirmation dialog. Holding Delete is the safeguard.

## Visual language for every screen

One instrument, one set of parts.

- Field: black.
- Ink (`#E8EAED`) for text and heavy ticks; a dim gray for secondary text; a darker gray for fine ticks and for anything switched off.
- Blue (`#2F8BFF`) for the heading pointer and for anything that is on or can be acted on: a switch that is on, the scroll position, the current screen's tick.
- Red (`#FF3B30`) for north, for failures (a save that failed, magnetic interference), and for deleting. Nothing else is red; the `Hold flat` and `Calibrate` prompts are light gray.
- Colors from the watch: on a watch whose system provides a color scheme taken from the watch face, the accent (the pointer and everything described as blue here) uses that scheme's primary color, and north uses a second color from the same scheme (its tertiary color), chosen so that north and the pointer are plainly different from each other. Failures and deleting use the scheme's error color. On a watch that provides no scheme, the accent is the blue above and north, failures and deleting are the red above. A waypoint's color is made from these three colors on every watch (next but one line).
- Wherever this document says "blue" or "red", read it as the accent, the north color or the error color as defined here.
- A waypoint's own color for that waypoint only: its line, its arrow, its row tick, the dot beside its name, its mark on the color wheel. It is the color at the waypoint's place on a ring that fades from the accent to the north color, on to the error color and back to the accent (2026-10-12; before that it was a hue, `Color.hsv(hue, 0.65f, 1f)`). See "Waypoint colors from the ring, as built".
- Breadcrumbs share one muted blue-gray so they never compete with waypoints.
- Every screen other than the compass is framed by a thin static ring of ticks at the edge of the display. The frame carries information where it can: scroll position as a blue arc, the color wheel on the edit screen, accuracy on the calibration screen, and three ticks at the bottom showing which of the three screens is in view.
- A switch is drawn as a tick at the right of its row: long and blue when on, a short gray stub when off. No pill-shaped buttons and no gray button backgrounds.
- One type family (the system's), light weight for large numerals, regular for labels, medium for a row that is on.
- Rows are text on black. Touch targets stay at least 48 dp tall even though the drawn marks are small.

## Compass

### Markers

- Every target is drawn as a line across the tick ring at its bearing, a little longer and heavier than a heavy tick.
- The focused target's line is in the waypoint's full color, with a thin black casing so it reads against the ticks.
- Every other target's line is a soft tint: a paler version of its own color (the same hue with about 60 percent of the saturation, and the same brightness except where it has to be brighter to read on black) at 60 percent opacity, with no casing. Only the focused one has its full color. How far targets can be told apart by color depends on the watch's colors (see "Waypoint colors from the ring, as built").
- The focused target also has an arrow: a triangle of the same shape and size as the blue heading pointer, in the waypoint's color, just inside the ring at the target's bearing, pointing outward.
- The dot markers are gone.
- A target within the arrival distance (under 10 m, or under 30 ft with feet and miles) has no line and no arrow: the direction to a point the wearer is standing on means nothing, and the readout says `Arrived at target`. Such a target is not faced. A waypoint that has just been logged therefore shows its line only once the wearer has moved away from it.
- Breadcrumbs, when a trail exists, are short muted ticks inside the ring. While retracing, the crumb being walked to is drawn like a focused target, in the breadcrumb color.

### Facing the target

- "Facing" means the shown heading is within 2 degrees of the focused target's bearing. Once facing, it stays so until the difference exceeds 3 degrees, so sensor noise at the edge does not make it flicker.
- Facing is never reported while the hold-flat state applies, or while the dial is still catching up after the app opens, the screen wakes, hold-flat ends or the north reference changes. It resumes once the dial has caught up, which takes at least 0.4 seconds.
- When the focus moves to another target, that target starts from not facing: it has to be within 2 degrees to count as faced.
- While facing, the focused target's line is drawn clearly thicker. It sits at 12 o'clock under the blue pointer at that moment, so the wearer sees one heavy line in the target's color there. There is no ring or circle.

### Logging with one press

- A single press of the side button saves the current position as a waypoint without leaving the compass.
- While it works, the distance slot shows a short status: `Saving…`, with the current accuracy once a fix is arriving; then `Saved Waypoint N` with the accuracy on the line under it, for about two seconds, with a light confirming tap; or a failure in red for about three seconds: `No GPS fix`, `Location is off`, `Needs access to precise location` (on two lines), or `Couldn't save`.
- The first press on a fresh install shows the system's location permission dialog; if granted, the save continues without a second press.
- The rules for which fix is saved are unchanged (15 m or better at once; the best fresh fix after 20 seconds), with one tightening: a fix taken before the press is never saved.
- A second press while a save is in progress does nothing.
- `Saved Waypoint N` is in a soft green, the confirmation color. The accuracy under `Saving…` and `Saved Waypoint N` is small and dim, in the chosen units. `No GPS fix` has `Try again outdoors` under it in the same small dim text; `Needs access to precise location` is on two lines, both in the error color; the other failures are one line.
- With no targets the status appears alone under the heading and the compass is clean again when it clears.
- The separate logging screen is removed.

### Getting around

- Three screens sit side by side and wrap around: settings, compass, waypoints. From the compass, swiping left shows waypoints and swiping right shows settings; swiping the same way twice reaches the other one.
- Long-pressing the compass also opens waypoints.
- Tapping the compass does nothing.
- Swiping up or down, or turning the crown, on the compass still moves the focus among the targets.
- The side button acts while the compass or the waypoints screen is in view, and logs a waypoint on either. It does nothing on settings, on the edit screen or on calibration.
- Pressing the crown leaves the app. A right swipe does not, and neither does Back: on the three screens Back turns one screen toward settings, as a right swipe does.
- Screens opened from a row (edit a waypoint, calibration) go back with the usual right swipe, and with Back.

### Unchanged

The clean compass with no waypoints, `MAG`, the hold-flat animation, tick feedback, the keep-awake rule, and the calibrate prompt.

## Waypoints screen

- A header line states how many waypoints are targets, for example `3 of 4 targets`. Tapping it sets all as targets, or clears all if all already are.
- First row: `Log here`, which saves a waypoint exactly as the side button does. It exists because not every watch passes its side button to apps.
- The side button saves a waypoint from this screen too, while it is in view.
- One row per waypoint, newest first: its tick (long and in its color when it is a target, a short gray stub when not), its name, and its distance when a position is known.
- Tap a row: set or clear it as a target.
- Long-press a row: edit it.
- While a breadcrumb trail exists, a row for the trail shows its length, and long-pressing it clears the trail.
- While a breadcrumb trail is being laid, a row `Drop crumb` under the trail's row drops a crumb, as a single press of the side button does then (added 2026-10-13; see "Changed by the owner after approval").
- With no waypoints: `Log here` and one quiet line saying there are none yet.

## Edit a waypoint (one screen)

- The frame is the color wheel: the ring the waypoints' colors come from, with the accent at 12 o'clock, the north color at 4 o'clock and the error color at 8 o'clock. Dragging along it, or turning the crown, changes the waypoint's color at once; there is no separate color screen and no Done button. The change is saved as it is made.
- The name is in the middle. Tapping it opens the system's text input.
- A target tick above the name shows and toggles whether it is a target.
- `Hold to delete` at the bottom: holding it for about a second deletes the waypoint, with the frame draining as it is held so the wearer can let go to cancel. No dialog.
- A right swipe goes back, from the edge of the screen too: a touch that goes down on the color wheel and moves toward the middle is a swipe and changes no color. (This replaces "a right swipe that does not start on the color wheel goes back".)

## Settings screen

One tap per switch, in this order: True north; Feet and miles; Tick feedback; Blind navigation; Breadcrumb trail; Show coordinates; Back up waypoints; then Calibrate (showing the current accuracy level, opening calibration) and the version.

New settings: Blind navigation (on by default) and Breadcrumb trail (off by default).

## Calibration screen

The figure-8 guide stays. The frame fills in green by quarters as accuracy improves (none for unreliable, one quarter for low, three for medium, all four for high). The accuracy level is the large text; the field strength is small; the interference warning is red, and while it shows the whole frame is filled in red.

## Blind navigation

- A setting, on by default (the owner: "enable blind navigation by default").
- When on, and a focused target (or the crumb being retraced) has a bearing: one strong pulse at the moment the wearer turns to face it, then a soft pulse about once a second for as long as they keep facing it.
- Silent while hold-flat applies, while the compass is not the screen in view, and when there is no focused target.
- Silent while the position the bearing comes from is old: the same condition that marks the distance `(old)`, a fix more than 20 seconds old. The heavy line is not affected. A new fix arriving while the wearer faces the target counts as turning to face it: one strong pulse, then the soft ones.
- Uses the standard haptic feedback call, with no vibration permission.
- Tick feedback continues to work alongside it.

## Breadcrumb trail

- A setting, off by default. While it is off, a double press means nothing special and a single press saves a waypoint at once.
- While it is on:
  - A double press with no trail starts one. The compass shows `Trail` and the number of crumbs.
  - While laying a trail, each single press drops a crumb at the current position, using the same fix rules as saving a waypoint. Named waypoints can still be saved from `Log here`.
  - A double press while laying switches to retracing. The newest crumb becomes the thing being walked to: its direction and distance are shown as for a focused target, labeled with its place in the trail.
  - On arriving at a crumb (within 10 m, or 30 ft), it is deleted and the next newest takes over. After the last one, the compass shows `Trail complete` briefly and returns to normal.
  - A double press while retracing returns to laying, keeping the crumbs that remain.
  - While a trail is being retraced, the waypoint focus is suspended and returns afterwards. Waypoint targets stay drawn as lines.
- Crumbs are stored separately from waypoints, survive closing the app, and are never shown in the waypoint list except as the one trail row.
- Location runs while a trail is being laid or retraced and the compass is in view, and the screen stays awake while retracing, as it does for a target.
- Whether a given watch delivers a double press of its side button to apps is not known. If it does not, the trail is started and switched from the trail row on the waypoints screen instead; that row offers the same actions. Whether it delivers a single press is not known either: the `Drop crumb` row drops a crumb without it.

## Coordinates and long names

An optional readout of the current position to four decimal places with hemisphere letters, and back-and-forth scrolling for names that do not fit. Both are built on the redesigned screens.

### Coordinates, as built

- With `Show coordinates` on, the compass shows the watch's position as two small lines: the latitude at the top of the readout, above `MAG` and the trail's label, and the longitude at the bottom, under the prompts. Each is in degrees to four decimal places with its hemisphere's letter and no sign, as in `43.7022° N` and `72.2896° W`. One step of the fourth place is about 11 m of latitude.
- The fourth place is rounded half up on the number as it is written in decimal: 43.70225 is `43.7023° N`. The letter is the rounded number's, so what rounds to zero is `0.0000° N` or `0.0000° E`. The antimeridian is `180.0000° E` from either side. The text is the same in every language setting of the watch.
- The lines are in the dim gray, at 0.070 of the dial radius. With the setting on the readout's rows stand, in dial radii from the middle: latitude 0.345 above; `MAG` and the trail's label 0.27 above; the name 0.185 above; the heading in the middle; the distance or a status 0.167 below; a status's second line 0.267 below, and `Calibrate` or `Hold flat` 0.275 below; longitude 0.351 below; the three ticks that show which screen is in view under it. These places were chosen during the build and changed on 2026-10-13 (before that: 0.170, 0.258, 0.262 and 0.348), when a row with a descender (`No target`, `Arrived at target`, `Locating…`, `Saving…`) was seen to touch `Hold flat` under it; they are in `nav/ReadoutRows.kt`, with a test that no two rows' ink comes closer than 2 px on the smaller round screen. `Hold flat` keeps the place of `Calibrate` although the coordinates are left out while it shows. With the setting off the rows are where they were (0.31, 0.195, 0.195, 0.30 and 0.31). Which set it is depends on the setting alone, so nothing moves while the compass is in use. The heading is the same size in both and stays pinned in the middle.
- The coordinates are left out only while the hold-flat state shows. They stay, both lines, through a save from `Saving…` to the end of `Saved…`, through every failure, for as long as `Calibrate` shows, during and after a swipe, and with `MAG` and `Trail N`.
- One function decides every line of the readout that says something about position (`readout` in `nav/Readout.kt`): the row under the heading, the smaller row under it, and the two coordinate rows. No fact is said twice and no two lines differ about one fact:
  - Before the first fix, `Locating…` is said once: by the distance where a target has the focus, and otherwise in the longitude's row. A press that has just failed for want of a position (`No GPS fix`, `Location is off`, `Needs access to precise location`) has said it, and nothing is added.
  - An old fix (more than 20 seconds, the condition that marks the distance) is marked ` (old)` once: on the distance where one is shown, and otherwise after the latitude. Old coordinates are in the fainter gray either way.
  - `Location is off` is said once: by a press's status, by the focused target's row, or, with nothing focused, in the longitude's row. Whether location is on is looked at when the app comes back on screen.
  - Without the precise location permission there are no coordinates and nothing in their place, also when the switch is stored on and the permission has been taken away in the system's settings; location is not asked for.
- A focused waypoint that has no distance while no position can come at all now says why in the row under the heading, in the error color, as the crumb walked to already did: `Location is off`, or `Needs access to` over `precise location`. Before, it read `Locating…` for as long as that lasted. This holds with the setting on or off. A waypoint that still has a distance from before location was switched off keeps it, marked old. The two-line form gives way to `Hold flat` and `Calibrate` (see "Decided during the build").
- Location: with the setting on, location runs while the compass is on screen (at rest in view or passing during a swipe) and the permission is held, with or without a target; this is a third reason beside targets and the trail in the one place that decides it (`locationWanted`). It is no reason to keep the screen on. With the setting off nothing changes. Seen on the API 35 emulator in the system's report of the GPS provider's request: off with the setting off and no targets, on (every 5 s, for the app) with the setting on and no targets, off again with the permission revoked.
- With the setting off and no waypoints the compass shows the dial and the heading only, as before.
- The calibrate prompt's tap area starts at the top of its word in both sets of rows (0.055 of the radius above the word's middle) and reaches 0.52 of the radius down from there, 48 dp at least. (v3 Task 8: with the coordinates on the word stands above the heading and the area reaches up from it; see the v3 spec.) The longitude under the word is drawn text and takes no touch.
- `Calibrate` together with the coordinates could not be brought about on the emulators, which report only high accuracy. Its place was looked at on both screen sizes with a temporary build that showed the prompt regardless of accuracy. After the change of 2026-10-13 its clearance was worked out, not seen: `Hold flat` is drawn in the same style at the same place, and its measured top and bottom, with the longitude's measured top, leave 2 px between a descender over it and its top and 4 px between its bottom and the longitude on the large round screen, and 4 px and 3 px on the small one.

### Scrolling names, as built

- A waypoint's name that is too long for its place slides back and forth in it. It waits 1.2 seconds at its start, slides at 30 dp a second until its end shows, waits 1.2 seconds, slides back at the same speed, and starts over. It does not wrap around. The place does not move or change size; the text is cut off at its edges and fades out over at most 8 dp at an edge it runs on past (never over more than it is too long by). There is no ellipsis while it slides.
- A name that fits is drawn as before and does not move.
- Where: the name above the heading on the compass; a waypoint's row on the waypoints screen; the name on the edit screen. On the edit screen the name is first drawn smaller on up to two lines, as before; a name too long for two lines at the smallest size is on one line at that size and slides. A tap on it opens the text input as before, and the area that takes the tap is unchanged.
- The first line of a logging status (`Saved Waypoint N`) is drawn smaller first, down to three quarters, as before; if it is still too wide it slides at that size. A result is shown for about two seconds, so of a line that long only the start and the beginning of the slide are seen.
- With the system's animations removed nothing slides, and a name that does not fit ends in an ellipsis, as before.
- A name slides only on a screen that is at rest in view with the app in front. While the screen is passing in a swipe, or something of the system's is over the app, the name stays where it is and goes on from there afterwards. On a hidden screen it stands at its start. A new name starts from the first wait. While a name waits at an end, or is held, no frame is asked for. The rule is one pure function (`marqueePhase`).
- Sliding composes nothing and measures nothing: the offset is applied to the sliding text's own layer. A name that fits has no layer and no clip of its own. Counted on the API 35 emulator with a temporary counter, over 20 seconds of a name sliding on the compass: the compass was composed 4 times (once for each position fix), the name not at all, and it was measured 0 times.

## Removed

The logging screen; the separate color screen; the delete confirmation dialog; the dot markers; opening the list by tapping the compass; leaving the app by swiping right; the All and None buttons (replaced by the header tap).

## Data

- The database gains a table for crumbs. This is the first schema change: it ships with a real migration from version 1, and the schema is exported from now on. Existing waypoints are kept. A destructive fallback is never used.
- Crumbs are in the same database file, so the existing backup rules and the deleted-content guarantees apply to them as they do to waypoints.

## Decided during the build, reported to the owner

- While the logging status has a second line, that line takes the place of the words `Hold flat` or `Calibrate`, and the calibrate prompt's tap area goes with its word. The hold-flat drawing keeps looping, and the words return when the status clears. That holds for the status of a press, which ends by itself. What a focused target says while no position can come (`Needs access to` over `precise location`) stands for as long as its cause does, and it is the one that gives way: while `Hold flat` or `Calibrate` is due, neither of its two lines is shown, the row under the heading has the target's distance if it has one and otherwise nothing, and the message is back when the prompt ends. `Location is off`, which is one line and not in the prompts' place, stays. So the order in that place is: the second line of a press's status; then `Hold flat`; then `Calibrate`; then the second line of a target's standing message. One function decides it with the rest of the readout (`readout` in `nav/Readout.kt`).
- A press while a result is waiting or showing starts a new save.
- A status line that does not fit its slot at full size is drawn smaller, down to three quarters, before it is cut short or, where animations are not removed, slides (see "Scrolling names, as built"). `Needs access to precise location` is too long for one line of the status at three quarters, so it is on the status's two lines, `Needs access to` over `precise location`, both in the error color: the first at the size of any status and the second at the size of the smaller line. Both are whole on both screen sizes, on the compass and on the waypoints screen's band, whose height (0.58 of the radius, the same as for `No GPS fix` with its second line) and place are unchanged: over a list with rows it covers the header, a drag that starts on it scrolls the list, and it stays where it is. In the settings the same two lines stand under `Show coordinates`, in the error color at the size of a row's smaller line; they are whole with the row in the middle of the screen, a quarter of the screen above it and a quarter below, on both screen sizes. The row is taller by that line while it shows, and the rows around it keep their places in the list.
- The save continues when the wearer moves to another screen. A result is shown for about two seconds for a save and about three for a failure, on the compass or on the waypoints screen, and that time counts only while one of those two is at rest in view. A result that arrives while neither is waits, and is shown for its full time on whichever of the two is in view next; a showing that is cut short starts again there. The confirming tap is given once.
- The screen stays on while a save is in progress, with or without targets.
- The confirming tap is given once, when the saved result is first shown.
- The first press asks for precise and approximate location together. If only approximate location is granted, nothing is saved, the compass shows `Needs access to precise location`, and the next press asks again.
- `Log here` on the waypoints screen starts the same save and stays on the waypoints screen. While a save is in progress or its result is showing, the status stands at the top of the waypoints screen, over the list and not part of it, so it is there wherever the list is scrolled to: the same words in the same colors as on the compass, with the second line small under them (dim when it is a note such as the accuracy or `Try again outdoors`, and in the error color when it is the second half of `Needs access to precise location`), on a black band that covers the rows passing under it. This holds for a save started from either screen. A touch that goes down on the band is not a tap or a long press on the row under it, and a drag that starts on the band still scrolls the list. The header keeps showing the count, and takes no tap while a status shows.
- The three screens: an app that is started afresh opens on the compass. An app that was left with the crown and is still running comes back on the screen it was left on.
- Back, on a watch that has it, turns one screen toward settings from each of the three screens and never leaves the app. Backs made close together each turn one screen.
- A screen opened from a row comes back to the screen it was opened from. If the system has stopped the app in the meantime and restores that screen, going back from it shows the compass.
- A swipe ends on a whole screen and turns one screen. A second swipe made before the first has come to rest, or a finger that catches the screens while they slide, carries on to the next screen. While the screens are being moved (a finger has them, or they are still sliding) no screen counts as in view: the compass acts on no crown turn, button press, vertical swipe or long press, gives no tick and no confirming tap, and does not count the time of a logging result. The words of a waiting result are drawn on the compass as it passes; its time and its confirming tap start when the screens have come to rest on the compass.
- A touch on the compass becomes one gesture: a sideways swipe turns to the next screen, a vertical swipe moves the focus, and a long press (the system's long-press time) opens waypoints with a light tap. A swipe at an angle goes whichever way the finger first travels far enough. A long press that starts on the calibrate prompt's tap area opens nothing.
- The compass acts on the crown and the side button only while it is at rest in view, and the waypoints screen acts on the side button only while it is at rest in view. A press reaches one screen, the one with the focus. While the screens are moving, a turn or press is dropped. The crown scrolls the list on the waypoints and settings screens.
- While another screen is in view the compass does not listen to the sensor, draws no frames, gives no tick, and does not count the time of a logging result. When it comes back the dial catches up and the ticks stay quiet until it has.
- The screen is kept on for targets only while the compass is in view. It is kept on during a save on any screen, the ones opened from a row included.
- Location for targets runs on all three screens while the app is on screen, as it did on the separate screens before: the waypoints screen shows distances from it.
- The three ticks that show which screen is in view are at the bottom edge on the waypoints and settings screens. On the compass they appear only around a swipe, from the moment the compass starts to move until about a second after it has come to rest, at the bottom of the readout (under the lowest line of text, inside the circle the letters sweep), where nothing on the turning dial passes.
- The window's own swipe-to-dismiss is switched off for the app, so no right swipe on the three screens can close it.
- The waypoints header reads `N of M targets`, with `target` when there is one waypoint (`1 of 1 target`, `0 of 1 target`), and `No waypoints yet` when there are none. With no waypoints the header is the quiet line and a tap on it does nothing.
- A waypoint's row has its name, its distance under the name when a position is known, and its tick at the right. A target's name is in ink at medium weight; the name of a waypoint that is not a target is dim. The header is dim and `Log here` is in the accent color.
- The rows of the waypoints screen keep their full size wherever they are, so a row is 48 dp tall at the edge as in the middle. The two ends of a row follow the round edge: a row above or below the middle is drawn shorter. Rows fade out toward the top and the bottom of the frame and are cut off inside it. The screen opens with `Log here` in the middle, the header above it and the newest waypoint below.
- The scroll position is an arc in the accent color on the frame at 3 o'clock, as long as the part of the list that is on screen. It is not drawn when the whole list fits.
- On the waypoints screen the three ticks that show which screen is in view stand in the frame's own band at 6 o'clock, and the frame's ticks leave a gap for them. The settings screen has the same frame, with its three ticks in the same place.
- A tap or a long press on the waypoints screen acts only while that screen is at rest in view, as on the compass.
- The edit screen has, from the top: the word `Target` with the tick that shows and switches it, in the waypoint's color; the name, with a dot in the waypoint's color beside it, on up to two lines and drawn smaller when it is long; and `Hold to delete` in the error color. Each of the three is 48 dp tall to the touch. The waypoint's color is marked on the wheel by a line across it.
- A touch "goes down on the color wheel" when it lands on the wheel's band (from 0.86 of the radius outward), on the rim outside it, or within 8 dp inside it. Such a touch is never a tap on what is under it and never holds `Hold to delete`, whose touch area reaches under the wheel at its corners. A touch that goes down anywhere else never changes the color, wherever it travels.
- What a touch on the wheel becomes is decided when it has moved far enough to be a drag. If it moves along the wheel at least as much as toward the middle, it picks the color under the finger for as long as it stays within 16 dp of the band; if it then leaves the wheel toward the middle, the color returns to what it was when the finger came down, drawn and stored, the rest of that touch changes nothing, and if it lifts more than half the screen to the right of where it began the screen goes back. If it moves mostly toward the middle from the start, it is an ordinary swipe: it changes no color and goes back like any right swipe. A touch that lifts without moving is a tap and picks the color it landed on, unless it rested longer than the system's long-press time, which changes nothing. Within three tenths of the radius of the center a finger picks no color.
- The crown turns the color by a quarter of a degree for each pixel of scroll, which is 32 degrees for one click of the emulator's crown.
- A color change is drawn at once. It is stored at most four times a second while it keeps changing, when the finger lifts, and when the screen is left, so the stored color is the last one drawn.
- Changes to waypoints (target, all targets, name, color, delete) are applied one at a time in the order they were made. Before this, a color or name change that overlapped another change to the same waypoint could undo it.
- `Hold to delete` takes one second. While it is held the color wheel gives its place to a ring in the error color that starts whole and drains toward 12 o'clock, leaving the frame's ticks behind it. Lifting the finger, moving it off the words' touch area or starting a swipe ends the hold: nothing is deleted and the wheel returns within about a fifth of a second. When the second is up the watch gives the long-press tap, the waypoint is deleted and the screen closes, once.
- A new name that is empty or only spaces keeps the old name.
- For a screen reader the heading is described with its name and unit, `Heading 236 degrees`, with `, magnetic` after it while `MAG` shows and `Heading unknown` while there is none; the row of three screens is described as a collection of three.
- For a screen reader: a waypoint's row is a switch with the actions `Set target` or `Clear target` and `Edit`; the header's action is `Set all as targets` or `Clear all targets`; the name's action on the edit screen is `Rename`; `Hold to delete` is described as `Hold to delete` with the waypoint's name and has the long-press action `Delete`; the wheel is described as `Color` with the waypoint's place on it as a number (`90 of 360`) and can be set. It names no color: which color is at a place depends on the watch.
- The settings screen is a list inside the frame, like the waypoints screen: a dim header `Settings`, a row for each switch, `Calibrate`, and a last faint line with the app's name and version and `MIT License`. It opens with `True north` in the middle and the header above it. The scroll position is the same arc at 3 o'clock, and the rows keep their 48 dp height and fade out toward the top and bottom.
- A switch's row has its label, a smaller line under it, and its tick at the right; the whole row is the switch. The label is in ink at medium weight when the switch is on and dim when it is off. The smaller lines are: `Off: magnetic north`, `Off: meters and km`, `Faint tap at each tick mark`, `Pulses when facing the target` (Blind navigation), `Double press starts a trail` (Breadcrumb trail), `GPS position on the compass`, `With this watch's backup`.
- `Calibrate` is in the accent color with `Accuracy:` and the level (`High`, `Medium`, `Low`, `Unreliable`) under it. That line shows the level the magnetometer last reported while the compass or calibration had the heading sensor running, and is empty until there has been one. The settings screen does not run the sensor.
- The rows of the settings screen reach the ring their ends follow, 10 dp further out on each side than the rows of the waypoints screen. A row in the middle of the screen shows both its lines whole on both screen sizes. In a row above or below the middle, where the ends come in, a line that would reach the tick fades out before it.
- A tap on the settings screen acts only while that screen is at rest in view, and no switch takes a tap until the stored settings have been read. A side button does nothing there. The switches wait for the settings file and for nothing else: not for the waypoints, the crumbs or a position. While the other screens wait because the crumbs cannot be read with Breadcrumb trail on (see "Breadcrumb trail, as built"), each switch shows its stored value and takes a tap, so Breadcrumb trail shows on and can be switched off, which ends that wait. Seen in a device test on the real activity with the `crumbs` table dropped as in the tests of a failed read: the row was enabled and on, its click switched the setting off, and the waypoints were shown again.
- Turning on `Show coordinates` without the location permission asks for precise and approximate location together, as the first save does. If precise location is not granted the switch stays off and the row's smaller line reads `Needs access to precise location` on two lines in the error color until the row is tapped again; if it is granted the switch turns on with no second tap.
- Blind navigation and Breadcrumb trail are stored with the other switches in the settings file. Where nothing is stored for them (a fresh install, an install updated from a build without them, a settings file that was found damaged and reset, a settings file that cannot be read) Blind navigation reads on and Breadcrumb trail reads off. Blind navigation's value is read by the compass (see "Blind navigation, as built"). Breadcrumb trail's value is read by the side button and by the trail (see "Breadcrumb trail, as built").
- The calibration screen has, from the top: `Move your wrist in a figure 8`, the figure 8 with a dot in ink going round it, the accuracy level as the large word in ink (`Unknown` until the magnetometer has reported one), and `Field:` with the field strength in whole microtesla under it. The frame's fill is the confirming green, starts at 12 o'clock, runs clockwise and takes about 0.4 seconds to move to a new level. A level other than the four the sensor defines fills nothing.
- The interference warning reads `Magnetic interference nearby. Move away from chargers, magnets and steel.` in the error color, in its own place under the field strength, and shows while the field is below 25 or above 65 microtesla. Nothing else on the screen moves when it comes or goes. While it shows, the whole frame is filled in the error color in place of the green, whatever the accuracy level, `Unreliable` and `Unknown` included; the accuracy word and the field line stay as they are, and when the warning goes the frame is again as full, in green, as the accuracy alone makes it. The emulators report only `High`, so the other levels are shown by unit tests of the rule (`calibrationRing`) and were not seen on a screen.
- The crown and a side button do nothing on the calibration screen. Back and a right swipe each leave it and show the screen it was opened from, with the settings list where it was.

- The heading sensor (the rotation vector, with the magnetometer for accuracy and field strength) runs while the app is on screen and either the compass is on screen (at rest in view, or passing during a swipe) or calibration is open. It does not run on the settings, waypoints or edit screens. It stops two seconds after it is no longer wanted, so going from the compass to calibration does not stop and start it. This is one decision (`headingWanted` in `nav/Panes.kt`), made above all the screens.
- The sensor runs while the compass is passing during a swipe as well as when it is at rest in view: the dial is visible then and is drawn live.
- Across a stop and a start of the sensor the accuracy level and the field strength shown are the last the magnetometer reported, until its first report after the start replaces them; a restarted sensor's first headings come before that report. Calibration therefore opens on the level it last showed, with no `Unknown` between, and interference left over from before a stop lasts no longer than the first new field strength.
- Under a screen opened from a row (edit, calibration) every one of the three screens is hidden in the sense used above: it takes no button, runs no frame loop, gives no tap and counts no time. Up to API level 35 the navigation library draws the three screens behind such a screen while it is dragged aside, as a picture; above it, nothing is drawn behind but black until the screen has left, and the three screens are built again only then. The picture drawn behind on those levels is of screens built at that moment and not yet running: a compass there shows `--` and a dial with north at the top until the screen above has left. The three ticks that show which screen is in view appear on the compass only when the row of screens has moved, not when a screen that covered the compass leaves.

### Colors from the watch, as built

- Three colors follow the watch: the accent, the north color and the error color. The waypoints' colors and their soft tints are made from those three and follow with them (since 2026-10-12). The confirming green, the ink and the grays and the breadcrumb color do not. There is no setting for this.
- The scheme is read with `dynamicColorScheme` of the Wear Compose Material 3 library the app already uses (1.7.1; no dependency was added). Its documentation (https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/wear/compose/material3/package-summary, "Added in 1.5.0") says it creates "a color scheme based on the current watchface", that "If the user changes the watchface colors, this color scheme will change accordingly", and that it "checks whether the dynamic color scheme can be used and returns null otherwise". The documentation names no Wear OS version. The 1.7.1 code returns a scheme only on API level 35 or above and only while the system's global setting `dynamic_color_theme_enabled` is 1; the scheme's primary is the system color `system_primary_fixed`, its tertiary `system_tertiary_fixed` and its error color `system_error_dark`. The Wear design guide (https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/styles/color/system) says "Each Watch face defines two seed colors, used for primary and tertiary palettes."
- Where the library returns no scheme, the accent is `#2F8BFF` and north and the error color are `#FF3B30`.
- What was seen, by image:
  - Wear OS 4 (API 33, `system-images;android-33;android-wear;arm64-v8a`) and Wear OS 5 (API 34): no scheme; the blue and the red.
  - Wear OS 5.1 (API 35, `system-images;android-35-ext15;android-wear;arm64-v8a`): the setting is not set and there is no scheme; the blue and the red. With the setting put to 1 through `adb` the library returns the image's fixed colors (primary `#D9E2FF`, tertiary `#FDD7FA`), of which the primary is too pale to use (saturation 0.149; see below), so all three colors are the app's own: the blue and the red.
  - Wear OS 7.0 (API 37, `system-images;android-37.0;android-wear-signed;arm64-v8a`, revision 1, the newest image available, on the AVD `Wear_OS_Newest`): the setting is not set as the image comes, and changing the watch face's color in its editor (Ocean, Sage, Forest) changes neither the setting nor the system's colors, so the app shows the blue and the red. With the setting put to 1 and a seed color written to the secure setting `theme_customization_overlay_packages`, both through `adb`, the image's own system builds a scheme from the seed and the three colors follow it. This is how the two schemes in `accent-a-large.png` (seed `2F8BFF`) and `accent-b-large.png` (seed `00AA55`) were made.
  - No image was found on which the watch face itself changes the scheme, and no physical watch was tested.
- A color from the scheme is used as it is unless one of these applies; the rules are pure functions in `nav/WatchColors.kt`:
  - Contrast: a color with less than 4.5 to 1 against black (the ratio the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines ask of small text) is mixed with white just far enough to have it, which keeps its hue.
  - Gray: a color whose saturation is under 0.15, as it stands or once lightened, is not used. If that is the scheme's primary (a near-black or gray one), the scheme is not used at all and all three colors are the app's own. If it is the tertiary or the error color, only that role has the app's red.
  - North apart from the pointer: if north's hue is less than 60 degrees from the accent's, it is turned to 60 degrees from it, the way round it already leans (clockwise when the two are the same). Of the seven schemes seen on the Wear OS 7.0 image, the system's own pair was the same color in one, 32 to 57 degrees apart in five, and 154 degrees apart in one.
  - North among the ticks and apart from the pointer: on the color that is drawn, in whole red, green and blue values, north's saturation is at least 0.5, its hue is at least 60 degrees from the accent's, and its contrast with black is at least 4.5 to 1. A tertiary that already meets all three is used as it is. Otherwise its hue is turned to 60 degrees from the accent's, it is given 0.5 of saturation, and it is made brighter until it reads; one that is too dark even at full brightness is made paler, which stops above 0.5 at every hue. The scheme colors seen had 0.19 to 0.31 in six of those seven schemes, and a north that pale was hard to pick out from the ink ticks beside it. The accent and the error color are not deepened.
  - The error color apart from the accent: an error color that is less than 60 degrees of hue and less than 0.3 of saturation from the accent is replaced by the app's red `#FF3B30`. With a pink scheme the two were `#FFBEC7` and `#FFBEB9`, 13 degrees and 0.02 apart, and `Log here` and `Hold to delete` differed only in their words. The error color is not compared with north.
  - The app's own blue and red pass through these rules unchanged.
- As first built a waypoint's color was a hue of its own that no scheme changed. Since 2026-10-12 it is made from the three colors (see "Waypoint colors from the ring, as built").
- When the colors change: the app reads the scheme when its screen is created and each time it comes back on screen. While the app is on screen a change of the setting alone is not followed until the wearer leaves and returns. When the system changes the scheme's colors it restarts the app's screen, whether the app is on screen or not; the new colors are shown at once, and the row of three screens is back on the compass.
- Turning the dial records nothing and composes nothing, as before. Coming back to the app records the dial once, as before, in the colors of that moment.
- On the Wear OS 7.0 emulator image the three screens answer swipes, swipes from the left edge and Back as on API 35, and no swipe closes the app; the crown and the side button reach the compass after each way of arriving on it.
- Leaving the edit and calibration screens by a right swipe: up to API level 35 the navigation library swipes its screens back itself. Above it (Wear OS 6 and later) the library only listens for the system's back gesture, which the app's window has switched off so that no swipe on the three screens can close the app; as first built, a right swipe therefore did not leave either screen on the Wear OS 7.0 image. Above API level 35 the app now puts those two screens in a swipe box of its own (the same one the library uses on the older levels). On the Wear OS 7.0 image 10 of 10 right swipes left the edit screen with the color unchanged and 10 of 10 left calibration, Back left both, a drag along the wheel still changed the color, a hold that started on the wheel deleted nothing, and holding `Hold to delete` deleted the waypoint and closed the screen once; on API 35 the same checks gave the same results.

### Waypoint colors from the ring, as built

- A waypoint's color is the color at its place on a ring of three colors: the accent at 0, the north color a third of the way round, the error color at two thirds, and the accent again at the full turn (`ringColor` in `nav/ColorRing.kt`). The place is the number from 0 to 360 stored with the waypoint, the same number that used to be its hue; nothing stored changed, and the database is as it was. A waypoint logged before this change keeps its number and has the ring's color for it.
- Between two neighbors the fade is straight in each of the red, green and blue values as they are stored (sRGB), which is how the mockups the owner chose from were drawn. It is not a fade in light or in a perceptual color space: half way between two colors is half way between their channel values, and can be darker than both.
- Two anchors that are the same color give that color for the whole third between them. On a watch with no scheme the three are blue `#2F8BFF`, red `#FF3B30` and red `#FF3B30`: the ring fades from the blue to the red over its first third, is that red from 120 to 240, and fades back to the blue. The way back is the way out reversed, so two places the same distance either side of the red third have the same color.
- The soft tint is made from the same ring color: the brightest of its three channels stays and the other two keep 0.40/0.65 (about 62 percent) of their distance below it, which keeps the hue and the brightness and leaves that share of the saturation; it is drawn at 60 percent opacity. For the colors waypoints had before, this gives the tint they had.
- A soft line has at least 3 to 1 against black as it is drawn (at 60 percent over black). Where the tint alone would have less, it is made brighter by the least whole step that reaches 3 to 1, every channel raised in the same proportion, which keeps its hue and its saturation; a tint that already has 3 to 1 is not changed. With no scheme the tint alone was under 3 to 1 for about 109 of the 360 degrees (about 36 to 84 and 276 to 324, the ring's darker stretch between the blue and the red), 2.50 at its lowest; drawn, the lowest is now 3.0. With the two schemes seen on the Wear OS 7.0 emulator nothing is changed: their lowest is 4.55 (seed `2F8BFF`) and 5.41 (seed `00AA55`). The full color of a focused target is not changed. For comparison, the soft tint of the hue wheel had 3.45 at its lowest.
- One waypoint has one color everywhere: its line and arrow on the dial, its row's tick, the tick and the dot on the edit screen and its mark on the wheel all go through the same function.
- The colors follow the watch as the pointer does. The three colors are held as state and read where a color is drawn: on the dial, the layer of the other targets and the layer of the focused target are each drawn again, once, when one of the three changes, and nothing is composed again; turning the dial still draws and composes nothing. Seen on the Wear OS 7.0 emulator: a change of seed with the compass in front restarted the activity and the lines, the arrow and the name's dot were in the new scheme's colors; with the edit screen open, switching the system's dynamic color setting off, leaving for Home and returning gave the same edit screen, in the same process, with the wheel, tick, dot and mark in the blue and the red, and switching it on again gave the scheme's colors back the same way.
- Contrast with the black field, lowest anywhere on the ring: 4.52 to 1 with no scheme (about 65 degrees round, between the blue at 6.26 and the red at 5.92, and the same at about 295); 8.30 with the scheme the Wear OS 7.0 emulator made from the seed `2F8BFF` (accent `#B7CFFF`, north `#D57FFF`, error `#FFBEB9`); 13.28 with the one from the seed `00AA55` (accent `#BBEFC2`, north `#DAE974`, error `#FFBEB9`).
- What can and cannot be told apart, with waypoints at eight places 45 degrees apart (0, 45, ... 315), read from screenshots on both round sizes and from the color values:
  - No scheme: five colors for the eight. 0 is the pointer's blue exactly. 135, 180 and 225 are one red, which is also the red of north and of `Hold to delete`. 45 and 315 are one muted violet (`#7D6DB1`), and 90 and 270 one rose (`#CB4F64`). The blue, the violet, the rose and the red are easy to tell from each other, as full colors and as soft tints.
  - Seed `2F8BFF`: eight different colors, all pale. 0 is the pointer's color exactly. 90 and 135 (two orchids, 135 being close to north) are hard to tell apart, as are 225 and 270 (two pinks, 225 being close to `Hold to delete`). As soft tints on the dial, the one at 315 is also close to those at 0 and at 270, and is a near-white lilac not far from the heavy ticks' color; a soft line is still wider and longer than a tick.
  - Seed `00AA55`: eight different colors, all pale. 0 is the pointer's color exactly. 90 and 135 (yellow-greens, both close to north) are hard to tell apart, as are 225 and 270 (salmon pinks, 225 close to `Hold to delete`). As soft tints, the four from 0 to 135 run into each other in small steps, and 315 is close to 0 and to 270.
- New waypoints get their numbers as before, by golden-angle steps (0, 137.5, 275.0, 52.5, 190.0, 327.5, 105.0, 242.6, 20.1, 157.6, 295.1, 72.6, ...). So the first waypoint an install logs has the pointer's color exactly, on every watch. With no scheme, waypoints 2, 5 and 10 fall in the red third and are exactly the red of north and of deleting, and waypoints 7 (`#E5454A`) and 8 (`#FB3D34`) are near it: 5 of the first 12 are that red or close to it.
- On the dial a focused target with the pointer's color has an arrow like the pointer's in the same color, at its own bearing; one with north's color has a line the color of the north tick, wider and longer than it. The focused target's place in the list, its name and its distance still say which it is.
- The edit screen's wheel is this ring: the accent at 12 o'clock, north at 4 o'clock, the error color at 8 o'clock. Dragging and the crown move the waypoint's place as before (one click of the emulator's crown is 32 of the 360), and what is stored is the place. With no scheme, moving through the red third moves the mark and changes no color. For a screen reader the wheel is `Color` with the place as a number (`90 of 360`).
- The breadcrumb color is not on the ring and does not follow the watch.

### Blind navigation, as built
- Facing is the one state the heavy line shows: within 2 degrees of the focused target's place on the dial to begin, held until more than 3 degrees off. The pulses follow that state and nothing else decides them. The decision is three pure functions in `nav/Navigation.kt` (`nextBlindPulses`, `pulseBetween`, `tickClearOfPulse`), made in the compass's frame loop from values the loop already has; it composes nothing and allocates nothing.
- The strong pulse comes on the frame facing begins, the soft pulse each 1000 ms after it for as long as facing lasts. The soft pulses keep to the strong pulse's seconds: frames that do not divide a second do not make them drift later. A frame gives at most one pulse; after a gap between frames of any length the pulses missed are not made up.
- Silent: with the setting off; with an old position (below); with no focused target; with a focused target that has no bearing (no position yet, or arrived, within 10 m or 30 ft, where no line is drawn); in the hold-flat state; while the dial is catching up after a pause (at least 0.4 s after the app opens, the screen wakes or the hold-flat state ends); while the compass is not at rest in view (a swipe under way, another of the three screens, edit or calibration on top); while the app is not in front.
- The first pulse after any silence is the strong one. Coming back to the compass while still facing the target (from another screen, from Home, from a screen that slept, from the hold-flat state) gives the strong pulse again, as does switching the setting on while facing and moving the focus to another target that is faced too.
- With a position that is old (the distance marked `(old)`: the latest fix is more than 20 seconds old, `isStale` on `NavUi.fixElapsedMs`), the bearing is still the last one known and the heavy line still shows, but the pulses are silent: the mark is for the eye, and a wearer who is not looking cannot see it. The first new fix while the wearer still faces the target gives the strong pulse, then the soft ones; nothing is made up for the time in between. Seen on the API 35 emulator: with location switched off while facing, the last soft pulse came 20 seconds after the last fix and none followed; with location back, one strong pulse 4 seconds later.
- The position saved from an earlier session gives no bearing at all: it is used for the declination only, a target's bearing and distance come from a fix of this session, and until one arrives the target reads `Locating…`, has no line and gives no pulse.
- Both pulses are `View.performHapticFeedback`, with no flags and no permission: `CONFIRM` for the strong one and `CONTEXT_CLICK` for the soft one, on every API level. The tick tap is `SEGMENT_FREQUENT_TICK` from API 34 and `CLOCK_TICK` on API 33, as before.
- What those play. In the platform's own table (`PhoneWindowManager.getVibrationEffect` on API 33 and 34, `HapticFeedbackVibrationProvider` on API 35) `CONFIRM` is the click effect, `CONTEXT_CLICK` the tick effect, and the tick tap's constant the texture tick effect; `LONG_PRESS` is the heavy click. A device's system can replace entries. The Wear OS emulator images do: on API 33, 34, 35 and 37 the system recorded `CONFIRM` as two thud primitives 100 ms apart, `CONTEXT_CLICK` as the tick effect and the tick tap as the texture tick effect. The emulators' vibrator supports the click and tick effects only: there the heavy click of `LONG_PRESS` was recorded as ignored (`ignored_unsupported`, API 35), which is why the strong pulse is not `LONG_PRESS`, and on API 37 the tick tap was recorded as played with the tick effect in place of the texture tick.
- All three are touch feedback to the system. With touch vibration switched off in the watch's settings the system drops them: on API 33, 34, 35 and 37 each pulse was recorded as `ignored_for_settings` with `haptic_feedback_intensity` at 0, and played again once it was restored. The flag that asked to ignore that setting has been honored only for privileged apps since API 33 and is not used. The platform also drops touch feedback in battery saver mode (`VibrationSettings.shouldIgnoreVibration`, API 33 source).
- The system stops a vibration that is playing when the same app asks for another (recorded as `cancelled_superseded`). A tick tap that falls on the frame of a pulse, or within 400 ms after a strong pulse or 150 ms after a soft one, is therefore dropped and not kept for later, so it cannot cut the pulse short; a pulse that follows a tick tap closely stops the tap. Apart from that the tick taps go on while the pulses run.
- The confirming tap when a waypoint is saved is `CONFIRM` too, the same as the strong pulse.

### Breadcrumb trail, as built
- Presses. With the setting off nothing waits: a press is a single press when it is let go, and the save starts inside the handling of that release (13 ms after it in a test on the API 35 emulator). Two presses 9 ms apart with the setting off were two single presses; the first saved a waypoint and the second came while that save was in progress and did nothing. With the setting on, a press waits 300 ms from being let go. A second press that goes down within those 300 ms makes a double press when it is let go, however long it is held short of the system's repeat; a second press that is held or canceled leaves the first a single press. The times compared are the key events' own. After a double press nothing waits, so a third press starts over. In tests with real key events on the emulators, presses 151 and 200 ms apart were a double press, presses 451 ms apart were two single presses, and a lone press started its save 309 ms after it was let go. The decision is one pure function (`pressKind` in `nav/Trail.kt`).
- A press is acted on only while the compass or the waypoints screen is at rest in view, both when it is let go and, for a single press that waited, when its wait ends.
- The modes are off, laying and retracing. A double press, and a tap on the trail's row, do the same thing: off to laying (crumbs that are stored are kept and the trail goes on from them); laying to retracing; laying with no crumbs to off, since there is nothing to retrace; retracing to laying.
- A single press saves a waypoint while the trail is off or being retraced, and drops a crumb while it is being laid. `Log here` always saves a waypoint.
- A dropped crumb goes through the same status as a saved waypoint: `Saving…`, then `Saved Crumb N` in the confirming green with the accuracy under it and the confirming tap, N being the crumb's place in the trail; and the same failures. A press while a save is in progress does nothing, whichever kind either is. A press taken while laying whose fix arrives after the trail has been switched to retracing or off stores nothing and shows nothing.
- While laying, the compass shows `Trail` and the number of crumbs (`Trail 5`) in the breadcrumb color in the slot above the target's name, where `MAG` stands; when both show they are side by side, `MAG` first. Nothing else on the compass changes while laying, and the waypoint focus stays.
- Crumbs on the dial: a short tick in the breadcrumb color (`#8FA6BF`, one named value, `CrumbColor`; it does not follow the watch's color scheme) between the letters and the ring, at the bearing of each crumb from the latest fix. Ticks are drawn whenever crumbs are stored and a fix of this session is live, whatever the mode. A crumb the wearer has arrived at has no tick, by the same two distances as any arrival (gone under 10 m, back beyond 12 m). Ticks are placed at whole degrees and crumbs in the same direction share one, so there are at most 360 ticks however long the trail is.
- While retracing, the crumb walked to stands where the focused target does: a dot in the breadcrumb color and `Crumb N` above the heading, its distance below (`Locating…` before the first fix, `(old)` when the fix is old; where no position can come at all, `Needs access to precise location` on two lines or `Location is off`, in the error color), and on the dial the focused target's line, casing and arrow in the breadcrumb color, heavier when faced. N is the crumb's place in the trail: its number among the crumbs left, the oldest being 1, so it is also how many are left. No waypoint is focused meanwhile: every target is drawn as a soft-tint line, and the crown and vertical swipes move no focus. The stored focus is not changed, so the same waypoint is focused again afterwards.
- Blind navigation treats the crumb walked to as the focused target. Seen on the API 35 emulator: the strong pulse on facing the newest crumb and a soft one each second; when that crumb was reached and the next one, in the same direction, took over, the strong pulse again and then the soft ones.
- Arrival, for waypoints and crumbs alike, begins under 10 m (30 ft with feet and miles) and ends only beyond 12 m (36 ft), so a position that wavers at the line does not switch `Arrived at target` on and off. A target that has arrived has no line and is not faced until it is beyond the outer distance. With no live fix nothing has arrived.
- Reaching a crumb: each position fix is looked at once. While retracing, a fix within the arrival distance of the crumb walked to deletes that crumb, by its id, and the next newest takes over. Only the crumb walked to is looked at, so one fix removes at most one crumb and never an older one ahead of the newest; crumbs dropped within a few meters of each other go one per fix (about every 5 seconds). Because a crumb is deleted when it is reached, the 12 m distance matters for a crumb only if the deletion has not happened yet when the next fix comes.
- Two conditions on reaching a crumb, since deleting it cannot be undone. The fix has to be as accurate as a fix that is saved at once: a reported accuracy of 15 m or better, the one rule (`isGoodEnough`) that saving a waypoint or a crumb uses. A poorer fix inside the distance deletes nothing and does not show the crumb as arrived at: its direction and distance stay on the compass, and the fix's accuracy stands after the distance in the dim color, written as a save's accuracy is (`30 ft ±66 ft`, `9 m ±20 m`), so the wearer standing on a crumb that stays sees why. Nothing is added with a fix good enough, or with an old fix, which is marked `(old)`. And the compass has to be the screen at rest in view with the app in front: on settings, the waypoints screen, edit or calibration, during a swipe, at Home or under one of the system's own screens, the trail does not advance, whether or not location is running there for the targets. Arrival at a waypoint is shown whatever the accuracy, as before; it deletes nothing.
- After the last crumb the trail is off and `Trail complete` is shown in the confirming green where the compass shows a logging result, for about two seconds, counted like a logging result's time. It is shown on the compass only: it is dropped when the compass goes off the screen, so it never reaches the waypoints screen's band. It comes with no tap. If a save is in progress or its result is showing at that moment, that status keeps its place for its time and `Trail complete` is shown straight after it, once. `Trail complete` belongs to that moment: if the compass goes off the screen or the app is no longer in front before it has been shown out, or a new trail is started, it is dropped, showing or still to come, and does not appear on a later visit. Other results of logging still wait to be shown, as before.
- The trail's row on the waypoints screen stands under `Log here` while the setting is on or crumbs are stored. It reads `Trail` with a smaller line: `Off`, `Off, 3 crumbs`, `Laying, 3 crumbs`, `Retracing, 3 left`; its tick is long and in the breadcrumb color while the trail is laid or retraced. A tap does what a double press does. Holding the row clears the trail: once the touch has lasted the system's long-press time, the frame shows a ring in the error color that drains for one second and the row's smaller line reads `Hold to clear`; when the ring is empty the watch gives the long-press tap, every crumb is deleted and the trail is off. Letting go before that, or the touch becoming a scroll or a swipe, clears nothing and is not a tap, and the ring goes within about a fifth of a second. While it is touched the row shows a press the way the other rows do. For a screen reader it has the actions the tap would do (`Start trail`, `Lay trail`, `Retrace trail`, `End trail`) and `Hold to clear the trail`. With the setting off a tap does nothing and the hold still clears.
- Dropping a crumb has a row of its own (the owner's decision of 2026-10-13): while the setting is on and a trail is being laid, `Drop crumb` stands directly under the trail's row, in the accent color like `Log here`. A tap on it, while the waypoints screen is at rest in view, drops a crumb the way a single press of the side button does then: the same permission question, the same status in the band at the top, the same confirming tap and the same fix rules. The row is not shown while the trail is off or being retraced, or with the setting off, and its call saves nothing then (a tap that lands just as the trail stops being laid starts no save and shows no status), so the row never saves a waypoint: `Log here` does that, and the trail's row starts a trail. For a screen reader it is one button.
- Switching the setting off ends laying or retracing in the same write and keeps the crumbs; switching it on again does not take the trail up by itself.
- Storage: crumbs are the table `crumbs` of the waypoint database (id, latitude, longitude, accuracy, time), ordered by id, which only grows. The mode is in the settings file. Both survive closing the app: after a forced stop in the middle of retracing with three crumbs left, the app came back retracing `Crumb 3`.
- The database is at version 2. A version 1 file is migrated by adding the empty `crumbs` table; a file made by the build before this one, with three waypoints logged on an emulator, is kept as a test asset and comes through with every waypoint unchanged. The schemas of both versions are in `app/schemas`. There is no destructive fallback: a file of a version this build has no migration for is refused and left as it is.
- Backup: the crumbs are in the backed-up database file and the mode in the backed-up settings file, under the same `Back up waypoints` switch and the same conditions as the waypoints. A crumb that was reached or cleared is deleted through the same database, which overwrites deleted rows; tests search every database file for the eight bytes of a deleted crumb's latitude and longitude and find none. On the API 35 emulator's local backup transport, with a temporary build without the encryption requirement: a backup made while retracing, after one crumb had been reached, held the three crumbs left and none of the reached crumb's coordinates; after uninstalling, installing and restoring, the app had the three crumbs, the waypoint and the setting, and was retracing `Crumb 3`.
- Location runs for a trail while it is laid or retraced and the compass is on screen, at rest or passing during a swipe, so a swipe that is begun and let go does not stop and start the fixes. The screen is kept on while retracing with the compass at rest in view, and only while a position can come: the location permission is held and location is switched on, looked at when the permission is answered and each time the app comes back on screen. Without either the screen follows the system's timeout and the compass says which is missing where the crumb's distance would be. Laying alone does not keep the screen on.
- Number of crumbs: the trail has no upper limit. The crumbs are read from the database when the app starts and again when the table changes, and held in memory; a press, a turn of the crown and a position fix read no database. A read of the crumbs that fails is tried again after 1 second and then at doubling intervals up to 30 seconds, for as long as the app runs. Until a read has worked, what happens depends on the Breadcrumb trail setting. With the setting off nothing waits for the crumbs: the screens load and show no trail row until the read works, a press of the side button and `Log here` save a waypoint, the crown moves the focus, and a position fix does nothing for the trail. With the setting on the trail is not taken for empty, and the screens are as they are before the stores have answered (no waypoints listed, no targets on the dial, no location running for them) until a read works; that holds too when the setting is switched on while the crumbs are unread, and switching it off shows the waypoints again. The settings screen's switches are not part of that wait. What waits then, each time for at most 2 seconds, by the stored mode: with the trail off, nothing (a press saves a waypoint and the crown moves the focus, at once); while a trail is laid, the crown moves the focus at once, and a press of the side button waits for the crumbs before it looks for a fix, holds up no change to a waypoint, and ends in `Couldn't save`; while a trail is retraced, a press of the side button waits before it looks for a fix and ends in `Couldn't save`, and a turn of the crown waits and moves nothing, neither holding up changes to waypoints; in every mode a double press or a tap on the trail's row waits, holding up changes to waypoints (target, name, color, delete) meanwhile, and changes nothing. `Log here` saves a waypoint in every mode, and a position fix never waits. A failed read was brought about in a device test by dropping the `crumbs` table from the app's database file through a second connection while the app had it open: with the setting off the screen state loaded, the crown moved the focus within a second and a press saved a waypoint; with the setting on and a trail being laid a press ended in `Couldn't save` and `Log here` saved a waypoint, and once the table was put back the app's own retry read it and a press dropped `Crumb 1`. The view model is told that the app has left the front by the activity's lifecycle as it is paused, not by a recomposition, so a crumb is not consumed at Home. A press and a turn of the crown act on what the trail was doing when they were made: the settings store keeps the trail's stored mode and its switch in memory as it last read or wrote them, the two changes to them run one at a time and each records what the file holds before it returns, and the crumbs are in memory too, so the mode is decided before the press's own call returns. A change that returns after the press is not in it. The view model reads the two as it is made, so only a press in the moment before that read has ended waits for the file. A change to either is written to the file and put in memory as one step that the cancellation of whoever asked for it does not divide: once the write has begun, both happen. Each fix works out a distance and a bearing for every crumb on the main thread. Measured on the API 35 emulator with 500 crumbs, retracing: that work took 0.8 to 0.9 ms per fix (1.0 ms at most), against 0.2 to 0.3 ms with no crumbs, once about every 5 seconds; looking at the fix for the trail took 1.2 to 1.5 ms from start to end, waits included. Turning the dial through a full circle gave the same frame times with 0 and with 500 crumbs: in two runs each, a median of 17 or 20 ms and a 99th percentile of 18 to 36 ms, the slower run of each pair being once the one without crumbs and once the one with, and 0.1 to 1.5 percent of frames late. The dial was not composed again while it turned.
- A double press as two presses close together is what the emulators deliver. Whether a watch's system keeps a double press of its side button for itself was not tested; the trail's row does everything a double press does. The `Drop crumb` row drops a crumb while a trail is being laid, as a single press does then.

### After the final review, as built

What was built for the owner's answers of 2026-10-13 (quoted under "Changed by the owner after approval").

Readings of those answers made by the build's controller, which are rulings and not the owner's words:

- The coordinates stay split as built, the latitude at the top of the readout and the longitude at the bottom.
- The confirming tap of a save stays the same effect as the tap given on turning to face the target.
- "Menus" means the waypoints list, the settings, the edit screen and the calibration screen; the "main screen" is the compass.
- The `Drop crumb` row drops a crumb as a single press of the side button does while a trail is being laid, since a single press is what drops a crumb; the trail's row already does what a double press does. The row shows only while a trail is being laid and never saves a waypoint.

Drop crumb row: see "Breadcrumb trail, as built".

The screen and a target without a position (question 2): nothing was changed. The screen is kept on while the compass is at rest in view and at least one waypoint is a target, whether or not a position can come. Seen on the API 35 emulator with one target: with the location permission revoked (`Needs access to precise location` under the heading) and with location switched off (`Location is off`), the app's window had the keep-screen-on flag and the system held its screen wake lock for it, as with the permission held; with no target neither was there. A breadcrumb trail being retraced keeps the screen on only while a position can come, as before.

The crown on the compass: the focus moves by one target once the crown has scrolled further than 40 dp one way, which is 80 pixels on the emulators' screens, where one click of the crown is 128 pixels and so one step. Scrolls the same way add up; what is left over after a step is dropped. A scroll the other way drops what was gathered and starts from itself, so a crown that is turned back answers after one step's turn. (Before, the distance was 80 pixels on every screen density, and a turn back had first to undo what the turn before had gathered.)

Menus and the system's font size (question 5):

- The text of the waypoints list, the settings, the edit screen and the calibration screen is drawn at its size from the screen's radius times the system's font scale. The compass's text is sized from the dial's radius alone, as before, and is the same at every font scale. The question asked before backups are turned off follows it too. One function sets the menus' text (`instrumentText` in `ui/Instrument.kt`, by the rule `menuTextSize` in `nav/TextScale.kt`); no screen multiplies anything itself. The scaling is in proportion to the font scale for every size: the system's own rule, which above the default scales large text less than small text, is not followed.
- The font size setting of Wear OS on the API 35 and API 37 emulators has six steps: 0.94, 1.0 (the default), 1.06, 1.12, 1.18 and 1.24.
- A ruling for the owner to see: the scale is kept between 0.85 and 1.24. The upper end is the largest step the watch's setting has. The edit screen and the calibration screen keep everything in a fixed place, and at 1.24 they are full: on the 192 dp screen a name on two lines takes all 48 dp of its row, and on calibration the accuracy, the field strength and the warning stand directly under one another (`docs/screenshots/font-largest-calibration-interference-small.png`, `font-largest-edit-small.png`). A font scale above 1.24, which the watch's setting does not offer, is drawn at 1.24. The lower end is below the setting's smallest step and is a guard only.
- What gives way at a larger size: a row of a list is as tall as its text needs and never less than 48 dp. A switch's label may take two lines and its smaller line one more than it has at the default size, and on the waypoints screen the trail's smaller line and a waypoint's distance may take a second line (on the 192 dp screen `Laying, 3 crumbs` does at 1.24); in the middle of the screen every label and smaller line of the settings is whole at 1.24. As before, a row above or below the middle shows the start of a long line and all of it once it is scrolled to the middle, and a waypoint's long name slides. On the edit screen the name's row grows from 48 dp up to the room between the rows above and below it. On calibration the line that says what to do stands on its lower edge, so its second line is added above; the interference warning keeps its three lines and is drawn smaller, down to three quarters, where its words would need a fourth.
- At the default font scale the settings, the waypoints list, the edit screen and calibration are drawn as they were before this change (compared picture by picture on the API 35 emulator; on calibration only the moving dot differed).
- How the font scale is kept out of everything else: the app's whole composition is given a density whose font scale is 1 (`WithoutFontScale` in `ui/Instrument.kt`, around the app in `MainActivity`), so inside the app a size in sp is that many dp on every API level and for every kind of text, and the system's font scale is read once when the activity is made (`followFontScale`) and comes in only in `instrumentText`. This was done because the system's conversion is not applied alike everywhere: on the API 33, 34, 35 and 37 emulators at a font scale of 1.24 a line height made with the density's own conversion was drawn about 9 percent too tall (a row's words 46 pixels where 42 were asked for), and a text that makes itself smaller to fit drew its font size that way too. The compass's status line, which is such a text, is now the same at every font scale.
- Not looked at: font scales on a physical watch, a watch whose setting offers other steps, and the screens with a screen reader at the largest size.

Turning `Back up waypoints` off (question 4):

- An app has no call that deletes its stored backup. What was found with the system's local transport on the API 33, 34 and 35 emulators, the same on all three: two backups in a row leave one stored set, the second in the place of the first. A backup pass in which the app hands over nothing is rejected by that transport, which then holds nothing for the app; what Google's transport does with such a pass is not known. A pass in which the app hands over one small file succeeds, and its backup takes the place of the one stored before. Android's documentation of Auto Backup says: "Only the most recent backup is stored. When a backup is made, any previous backup is deleted." (https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup). The app does not decide how many backups are kept.
- So, as built: a tap on the switch while it is on opens a question on a screen of its own, in the style of the lists: `Turn off backup?`, under it `The next backup replaces the stored one, without waypoints.`, and under them the two answers side by side, each at least 48 dp tall: `Keep on` at the left in the accent color and `Turn off` at the right in the error color. The screen is a list, so at a font size where it does not all fit it scrolls. `Keep on`, Back and a right swipe leave the switch on. `Turn off` turns it off and records in the settings file that the wearer did. A tap on the switch while it is off turns it on at once.
- With the switch off by the wearer's doing, a pass of the system's backup, and a pass that is a transfer to another watch (the controller's ruling of 2026-10-13), is handed one marker file (`backup-switched-off` in the app's files folder, written for the pass and deleted after it) and nothing else: no waypoint, crumb or setting. With the switch off for any other reason (a damaged settings file is reset with it off; a file written before this record was kept) and with a switch that cannot be read, nothing is handed over, as before. Nothing is handed over either while the app's files folder takes no write: the pass tries a write there first. The decision is `backupPass` in `nav/Backup.kt`.
- The app cannot make a backup run; the system decides when one does. Until one runs, the stored backup is as it was. The encryption requirement of the backup rules (a screen lock, for the cloud) holds for the database and the settings file and not for the marker, which the agent hands over itself. Seen with the committed build on the API 33, 34 and 35 emulators (`scripts/backup-off-check.sh`, no screen lock): with the switch on and the transport not saying it encrypts, the pass handed over nothing; with the transport saying it encrypts, or saying the pass is a device transfer, the database was handed over; with the switch turned off through the question and the transport not saying it encrypts, the marker alone was handed over and the backup succeeded; and a pass that says it is encrypted, or a transfer, then handed over the marker alone as well (API 33, 34 and 35).
- Turning the switch on again clears the record, and the next pass copies the database and the settings as before. Seen on the API 35 emulator: off, backup (the stored archive holds the marker alone and the waypoint's name zero times); on, backup (the database and the settings file, the name once).
- Turning backups off holds across a restore (the controller's ruling of 2026-10-13 after a security review, which reverses the earlier one that a restore must leave backups on). A restore of the marker's backup brings back no database and no settings file, and the system does not write the marker to the app's files (it restores only the files the backup rules name); but the marker comes through the backup agent as it is restored, the agent puts that on record, and when the restore ends it writes the switch off, turned off by the wearer. So after the app is installed again on this watch, or set up on another from the backup or by a transfer, the switch is off without the question having been asked, and the next backup is a marker backup again. The record is two files, `backup-off-owed` in the app's files folder and in its no-backup folder, neither named by the backup rules; one is enough. If the settings file cannot be written at that moment the records stay, every pass takes the switch as off by the wearer while one does, the settings screen shows the switch off, and the write is tried again at each pass and each start of the app. A tap on the switch then turns it on as any tap on a switch that shows off does, and once that is written the records are removed, so it stays on. If neither record nor the settings file can be written, what is left is in memory only and ends with the process; a later pass hands over nothing for as long as the app's files folder still takes no write. Seen on the API 33, 34 and 35 emulators with the settings folder made unwritable (`chmod 500`) and the marker's backup restored over the app: both records there, the stored switch still on, an encrypted backup holding the marker alone, the app showing the switch off; with the folder writable again and the app started, the switch stored off by the wearer and the records gone. A restore of a backup made with the switch on brings the waypoints and the settings with the switch on. A restored settings file that says off stays off, and one that cannot be read counts as off, as before; nothing in the app turns the switch on at a restore. What is restored is the backup that is stored, and the app cannot make the system run a backup. So when the switch is turned off and the app is uninstalled before the system has made a backup with the switch off, the earlier backup is still the stored one, and installing the app again restores its waypoints and its settings, with the switch on. Seen on the API 35 emulator with the local transport: switch on, a backup; `Turn off` (stored off, by the wearer); no backup pass; uninstall, install: the waypoint was back, the backed-up settings were back, the stored file had neither switch key and the row showed on. `BackupManager.dataChanged()`, which the app calls when the switch changes, is not relied on to bring a pass forward: its documentation says "Note: This only works if your application is performing Key/Value backups." (https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/backup/BackupManager), and the app's backup is a full backup. Whether it has any effect was not tried. The same happens through adb without an uninstall: after `bmgr restore` of a backup made with the switch on, over an install where the switch had since been turned off, the stored file had neither switch key and the row showed on (API 35). The app has one record of a switch owed off for the whole process, which the backup agent and the settings screen both ask, and its settling and the wearer's own choice run one at a time, so the later of the two decides. The copy of a pass is decided a second time with the database locked, by the same function that decided the pass. Seen with the committed build on the API 33, 34 and 35 emulators (`scripts/backup-off-check.sh`): off, backup, uninstall, install: switch off, no database, next backup the marker alone with the waypoint's name zero times; on (one tap, no question), backup, uninstall, install: the waypoint back and the switch on.
- Not exercised: Google's backup service, a device-to-device transfer, and a watch with a screen lock.
